AMD Company Research Report

Information cutoff: October 13, 2026

Research entity: Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries

This report covers AMD's founding and business evolution, chips and software, customer and manufacturing relationships, five years of financial results, and the latest disclosed developments. AMD has developed from an early supplier of compatible components into a design company spanning CPUs, GPUs, programmable chips, and AI systems. Revenue was US$34,639 million in 2025; the latest reported financial quarter was Q2 2026, with revenue of US$11,536 million. Its revenue scope, reporting segments, and manufacturing model have changed several times, so historical figures must be understood alongside their periods and acquisition accounting scope.[F05][F07]

1 Founding and development

1969: A founding team from Fairchild

Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) was incorporated under Delaware law on May 1, 1969.[H01] The Computer History Museum (CHM) lists it among the Fairchild spin-offs founded in Sunnyvale, California, in 1969, recording that all eight cofounders had worked at Fairchild Semiconductor, including Jerry Sanders. Its initial strategy was to supply improved logic and analog integrated circuits corresponding to existing products from Fairchild and Texas Instruments.[H02]

Sanders' background was primarily in sales and marketing. CHM's archival catalog reproduces the abstract of his 2002 interview for Stanford's Silicon Genesis: he earned an electrical engineering degree in 1958, joined Fairchild in 1961, and later led worldwide sales; he was dismissed after Fairchild changed its management team.[H03] These career details come from the interview abstract preserved by the museum.

In CHM's Am2900 oral history recorded on February 11, 2009, cofounder Sven Simonsen recalled that Sanders and John Carey were dismissed after new management arrived at Fairchild, and that the other founders gradually came together and decided to establish their own company. He recalled total capital raised in 1969 of US$1.55 million and described AMD initially second-sourcing Fairchild's 9300 series of medium-scale integrated circuits before expanding into proprietary products.[H04] The funding figure is a cofounder's recollection forty years later, rather than an amount independently verified against contemporary accounts or offering documents.

The 1970s: Compatible components, proprietary designs, and customer support

AMD's early business extended beyond personal computer processors. Advertisements preserved by CHM show early sales of 64-bit RAM and marketing that emphasized commercial devices meeting the testing specifications required for high-reliability aerospace applications, with a slogan highlighting MIL-STD-883 testing. This describes the products and marketing of the period; it does not establish any particular aerospace company as a direct customer.[H05]

Proprietary products and second-source products coexisted during the 1970s. The Am2900 bit-slice processor family was an important proprietary product of this period: customers could combine multiple slices and control components to implement the processors they needed. John Springer, a participant in its development, recalled July 21, 1975, as the Am2901 announcement date. Clive Ghest recalled earlier visits to computer companies including Digital Equipment Corporation, Data General, and Honeywell to understand their system structures, with the aim of designing components for different commercial and military systems. Sales and application engineers subsequently explained the design approach through customer seminars.[H04] These names reflect participants' recollections of contacts during the 1970s, and are not evidence of customers in AMD's founding year, procurement contracts, or transaction amounts.

AMD became a public company in 1972. Its shares were listed on the New York Stock Exchange on October 15, 1979. Becoming a public company and moving trading to the NYSE were separate events; they should not be combined into a statement that AMD first went public in 1979.[H06][H05] CHM's historical advertising archive also records a second-source agreement with Zilog for the Z8000 in 1978, followed by a shift, by 1983, to producing the 8086/8088 family for the IBM PC under an agreement with Intel.[H05]

1982–1995: Intel technology cooperation and disputes

AMD's relationship with Intel included both cooperation and litigation. The contract reviewed in the California Supreme Court's 1994 opinion was signed in February 1982 to enable an exchange of technical information, allowing each party to become an alternative source for the other's products. The contract had a ten-year term, with termination possible after five years subject to the specified advance notice. Disputes subsequently arose over product exchanges, 80386 technology, and contractual duties; the opinion records that AMD's Am386 appeared in March 1991.[H07]

On December 30, 1994, the California Supreme Court reversed the Court of Appeal's contrary determination concerning the arbitrator's remedial authority, holding that the relief awarded in this case was within that authority.[H07] This report describes a specific arbitration and judicial review outcome; it does not characterize it as AMD prevailing in every intellectual property dispute or every subsequent antitrust dispute.

On January 11, 1995, the companies announced a settlement of all legal disputes then outstanding. The agreement granted AMD a perpetual license to use Intel 386 and 486 microcode, while specifying that AMD had no right to copy other microcode, including Pentium, P6, and 486 ICE. The announcement also stated that AMD would pay Intel US$58 million for past damages in the 486 ICE matter, while Intel would pay AMD approximately US$18 million, including interest, under the earlier arbitration award. Both parties would dismiss their pending court cases and appeals and negotiate a separate patent cross-license.[H08] This settlement does not mean that AMD's subsequent processors all used Intel designs.

1996–2003: NexGen, K6, Athlon, and AMD64

AMD completed its acquisition of NexGen on January 17, 1996, through a share exchange in which each NexGen common share was converted into the right to receive 0.8 AMD common shares.[H09] AMD's prospectus in August of that year disclosed that K5 had been introduced relatively late and had not yet achieved broad market acceptance. One purpose of the NexGen acquisition was to accelerate the introduction of next-generation processors, and AMD was combining NexGen's sixth-generation design with its own design, verification, and manufacturing technologies for K6.[H10] Its FY1998 Form 10-K subsequently confirmed that volume shipments of K6 began in the second quarter of 1997.[H11]

AMD introduced Athlon in 1999. Its 1999 annual report disclosed problems earlier that year with K6-2 yields and the distribution of speed grades, resulting in insufficient supplies of the high-frequency products some customers needed. Operating performance improved in the second half of the same year, but the full year still produced a net loss of US$88.936 million on revenue of US$2.8576 billion.[H12] These historical figures preserve the distinction between product developments and annual operating results; the launch of a single product does not establish sustained profitability. AMD also introduced the 1 GHz Athlon on March 6, 2000. This report records the model and release date without adopting the annual report's broad performance leadership claims, which were not independently tested for this research.[H13]

On April 22, 2003, AMD introduced Opteron for servers and workstations.[H14] Athlon 64 followed in September of the same year. AMD's 2003 annual report explained that both used AMD64 technology, extending the x86 instruction set architecture to 64-bit computing while retaining the ability to run existing 32-bit applications, with corresponding products for servers, workstations, desktops, and mobile computers.[H15] This technical description does not establish verified compatibility for every operating system or every legacy application in any configuration. Names such as IBM, Microsoft, and Oracle in AMD's announcement referred respectively to planned server products, operating system development, or software support commitments; these must be distinguished from procurement and delivery that had already occurred.[H14]

2005–2012: Ending flash-memory consolidation, acquiring ATI, and separating wafer manufacturing

AMD previously operated a flash-memory business. It established the FASL flash-memory joint venture with Fujitsu in 1993.[H11] On December 21, 2005, Spansion, previously a majority-owned subsidiary, completed its initial public offering, reducing AMD's ownership to approximately 38%. From that date, AMD ceased consolidating Spansion's operating results and financial position into its own statements. A further share sale in November 2006 reduced its year-end holding to approximately 21%.[H16] Comparisons of AMD's total revenue across this period therefore require attention to changes in consolidation scope.

On October 25, 2006, AMD completed the acquisition of ATI Technologies, adding graphics processors, chipsets, and consumer electronics products. The approximately US$5.4 billion transaction value in the completion announcement was measured using AMD's share price on October 24, 2006, and excluded assumed equity awards. Consideration included approximately US$4.3 billion in cash and approximately 58 million AMD common shares, with a US$2.5 billion term loan financing part of the cash consideration.[H17] The total purchase price recorded under acquisition accounting in the 2006 annual report was US$5.604 billion, including the value of vested options and restricted stock units and US$25 million of transaction costs.[H16] The two amounts have different definitions and should not be treated as contradictory figures. The completion announcement also introduced the Fusion initiative; the integrated CPU/GPU objective proposed at that time was not a product already delivered that day.

The manufacturing model also changed. AMD, ATIC, and related investment entities completed the transaction establishing a manufacturing joint venture on March 2, 2009. AMD contributed the German subsidiaries owning Dresden's Fab 30/38 and Fab 36, certain manufacturing assets, personnel, and intellectual property to The Foundry Company. It was renamed GLOBALFOUNDRIES on March 4. A separate wafer supply agreement arranged AMD's purchases of products manufactured by the company.[H18] On March 4, 2012, AMD announced an amended supply agreement and agreed to transfer its remaining GLOBALFOUNDRIES equity interest, ending its ownership.[H19] The 2009 asset separation and the 2012 equity exit arrangement were therefore different stages; ending equity ownership did not mean the wafer procurement relationship immediately ended.

The legal disputes also reached a new settlement during this period. AMD's November 17, 2009, Form 8-K records that the parties signed settlement and patent cross-license agreements on November 11. Intel was required to pay AMD US$1.25 billion, and AMD agreed to dismiss the litigation then pending in Delaware and Japan and withdraw its regulatory complaints worldwide. The joint announcement was released on November 12.[H20] This private settlement was separate from cases or rulings by independent regulators.

2014–2022: Leadership changes, Zen products, and adaptive computing

On October 8, 2014, AMD's board appointed Lisa Su president, chief executive officer, and a director, effective immediately, succeeding Rory Read. The announcement recorded that she joined AMD in 2012, previously held technology and business management roles at Freescale, and had worked at IBM for thirteen years.[H21]

On March 2, 2017, AMD began selling its first three Ryzen 7 desktop processors: the 1800X, 1700X, and 1700, each with eight cores and sixteen threads. The company described Ryzen as its first processor using the new Zen microarchitecture and stated that development had taken four years.[H22] The EPYC 7000 series of data center processors, using Zen cores and configurations of up to thirty-two cores, launched on June 20 that year.[H23] This report records the architecture, models, uses, and delivery status without generalizing the company's comparative tests into performance conclusions for all workloads.

AMD completed the acquisition of Xilinx on February 14, 2022, making it a wholly owned subsidiary. Each Xilinx common share was converted into the right to receive 1.7234 AMD common shares, with fractional shares handled as specified.[H24] The acquisition announcement stated that the all-stock transaction had originally been announced on October 27, 2020, and added technologies including FPGAs, adaptive SoCs, AI engines, and software.[H25] AMD's 2022 annual report recorded total purchase consideration of US$48.8 billion. The fair value of the common shares issued was measured using the share price on February 11, 2022, the trading day preceding completion. The annual report stated that Xilinx's results were consolidated from the acquisition date and reported in the Embedded and Data Center segments.[H26] The valuation at the initial announcement and the accounting consideration at closing must therefore also be distinguished.

2022–2026: Expanding networking and AI systems businesses

On May 26, 2022, AMD completed its approximately US$1.9 billion acquisition of Pensando, adding data processing units and software for networking, security, and storage services.[H27] On August 12, 2024, AMD announced that it had completed the approximately US$665 million cash acquisition of Silo AI, adding engineering teams for AI models and deployment.[H28] ZT Systems' design team was subsequently retained separately from the manufacturing business that was sold. Helios, Helion, and proposed acquisitions in 2026 are discussed in the product, manufacturing, and latest developments sections. Transaction prices in announcements do not automatically equal purchase accounting measurements.[OP31][OP13][OP15][OP32][OP33]

2 Corporate entity and revenue composition

Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is a Delaware-incorporated public company headquartered in Santa Clara, California. Its common shares trade on Nasdaq under the symbol AMD. This report covers the global operations of AMD and its consolidated subsidiaries. It designs computing chips and related software and sells through direct customers, equipment manufacturers, and distribution channels; design and wafer manufacturing are separate stages.[F05][OP29]

Since 2025, AMD's reporting segments have been Data Center, Client and Gaming, and Embedded. Data Center includes server CPUs, AI accelerators, and certain networking products; Client and Gaming includes PC processors, graphics cards, and semi-custom chips for gaming devices; Embedded includes programmable logic, adaptive SoCs, and embedded processors. Product brands do not map one-to-one to reporting segments: for example, both Instinct and Radeon are GPUs, but serve different markets and segments.[F05][OP01]

3 Uses of chips, products, and systems

AMD's products are easier to understand by distinguishing CPUs, GPUs, NPUs, and FPGAs. CPUs handle general-purpose computing and system control; GPUs perform parallel computing or graphics tasks; NPUs are specialized computing units for certain AI tasks; FPGA logic is configurable. Chips, accelerator cards, servers, and racks are also different delivery formats. Announcing a chip does not establish that a complete customer system is operational.

Helion, introduced on October 12, 2026, is a different rack blueprint for agentic AI, supporting sixth-generation EPYC CPU-only configurations and combinations of EPYC with MI350P. The announcement stated that partners were still developing products, with availability planned for November. As of this report's date, its status was introduced and in development.[OP15]

4 Software and development tools

ROCm provides runtimes, compilers, mathematical and communication libraries, and development tools for AMD compute devices, serving AI and HPC. Developers must check support by device, operating system, framework, and version. The October 5, 2026, release notes for ROCm Core SDK 10.1.0 cover Linux and Windows and identify technical previews and known issues. Support for a platform does not mean all components and graphics cards have identical support coverage.[OP17]

Programmable chips use a different development workflow. Vivado supports hardware design and implementation, while Vitis supports application and system development for FPGA fabric, Arm processors, and AI Engines. Vitis HLS can synthesize C/C++ functions into RTL. At the time of reading, the page listed version 2026.1 and described free development alongside certain implementation workflows requiring valid licenses. The product page provides no financial breakdown from which independent software revenue can be calculated.[OP18]

ROCm and Vitis address different compute devices and development tasks. Their public documentation supports statements about functionality and compatibility, but does not independently establish customer switching costs, performance in every application, or software business revenue. This report does not convert the number of open-source, licensed, or technical support products into an undisclosed scale of charges.[OP17][OP18]

6 Supply chain and manufacturing arrangements

AMD operates a fabless model, designing chips and relying on external wafer production, packaging and testing, and related supplies. Its 2025 annual report disclosed reliance on TSMC for microprocessor and GPU wafers at 7nm and below, with GlobalFoundries used for 12nm/14nm HPC products. Programmable logic also uses TSMC, UMC, and Samsung. Packaging and testing arrangements include two ATMP joint ventures associated with Tongfu, along with SPIL, KYEC, and others. A public supplier list does not establish that every supplier manufactures every model.[OP01][OP29]

The production process can be understood as chip design, wafer fabrication, packaging and testing, board or system integration, and channel delivery. HBM memory and advanced packaging for GPUs are separate supply stages from logic wafers. The Venice announcement in May 2026 described a TSMC 2nm production ramp in Taiwan and mentioned SoIC-X and CoWoS-L. Arizona production remained a future plan; the announcement did not provide complete capacity allocations or yields by product.[OP04]

Samsung announced a memorandum of understanding on March 18, 2026, covering primary HBM4 supply for MI455X and DDR5 for sixth-generation EPYC, while describing its existing primary HBM3E partnership for MI350X/MI355X. References to logic foundry work in the announcement were limited to discussions of opportunities. They do not establish that Samsung has taken on Venice logic wafer manufacturing.[OP30]

ZT Systems' system design and manufacturing businesses now have different ownership. AMD completed the acquisition in March 2025, retaining design-related IP and personnel. The data center infrastructure manufacturing business was sold to Sanmina on October 27, 2025, with the buyer's announcement confirming completion and describing a manufacturing partnership for new product introductions. This report does not continue to classify the complete manufacturing business that was sold as AMD-owned manufacturing capacity.[OP31][F05]

10 Markets and comparable companies

AMD operates across several computing markets: server and PC CPUs, AI accelerators, discrete graphics, programmable chips, embedded computing, and networking. Server CPUs can be compared with Intel and Arm platforms; AI accelerators involve NVIDIA and customer-designed ASICs; PC CPUs also include Intel and Arm platforms; discrete graphics includes NVIDIA and Intel; and programmable logic includes Altera, Lattice, and others. Companies have different business scopes and reporting segments, so dividing their total revenues does not produce a particular product's market share.[OP01][OP23]

This research did not obtain complete, publicly verifiable CPU, discrete GPU, or AI accelerator market share data with matching periods and metrics. It therefore provides product relationship descriptions and AMD's internal revenue composition without inventing a market share chart. Performance comparisons should specify product models, software, workloads, precision, power, and test conditions; this report makes no unconditional performance ranking.

11 Disclosed developments and current status

Export licenses directly affect the saleable scope of certain products and inventory accounting. AMD's Q2 2026 Form 10-Q disclosed that MI308 shipments to Chinese customers began under certain licenses in late 2025. Certain MI325 licenses were obtained in February 2026, but the filing still stated that AMD did not know whether China would permit imports. The licenses require inspection in the United States first, with a 25% tariff on the related imports for inspection. This report retains the filing's timing and does not conclude that sales of all restricted products have resumed.[OP02]

The same filing recorded that US officials expressed an expectation in August 2025 of receiving 15% of revenue from licensed MI308 sales to China, but that no regulations implementing the requirement had been published as of the disclosure. Official statements, formal rules, payments made, and completed sales are different evidence states; this report does not describe the percentage as a cost already in force and paid.[OP02]

External manufacturing and supplies of memory, advanced packaging, and components involve capacity, delivery, and product transitions. Customer adoption also involves equipment, software, and framework compatibility. Public materials do not establish complete procurement volumes, manufacturing costs, or yields by model, nor do they disclose all customer contract prices. This report retains those information limits without assigning its own risk probabilities or ratings.[OP04][OP17][OP29][OP30]

Recent acquisitions must be presented according to transaction progress. Although the August 6, 2026, Taalas headline used “Acquires,” the body described a definitive agreement still subject to closing conditions and regulatory approvals. This research did not obtain a subsequent completion announcement. On September 28, 2026, AMD announced an approximately US$8.2 billion all-stock agreement to acquire World Labs, expected to close by year-end and subject to approvals. The arrangement for Fei-Fei Li to join AMD's management takes effect after closing, so she cannot be listed as a current executive as of this report's date.[OP32][OP33]

12 Statistical methods and research coverage

The information cutoff and reading date are both October 13, 2026. Annual reports cover FY2021 through FY2025, and the latest actual financial period is Q2 2026. Product and transaction announcements are updated through October 12. The research uses regulatory filings, company announcements, product documentation, counterparty disclosures, and historical archives. Each source supports only its registered reading scope, with manufacturer statements, contractual plans, transaction completion, and actual customer deployment described separately.

Amounts are in US$ million by default. Fiscal year ends and week counts are shown in the tables; fiscal years are not mechanically equated with calendar years. Annual figures are audited GAAP data, while interim figures are unaudited. Gross margins retain the company's whole-percentage disclosures; other derived percentages are calculated from the original whole-number amounts and displayed to one decimal place. Year-over-year comparisons use the corresponding prior-year period; sequential comparisons use adjacent quarters; segment revenue shares divide segment revenue by consolidated revenue for the same period.

Total cash means cash and cash equivalents plus short-term investments; the main debt table uses net carrying amounts. Capital expenditure is limited to cash purchases of property and equipment. Continuing and discontinued operations, single-quarter and half-year periods, segment and consolidated figures, and internal revenue composition and market share have distinct labels. Data files retain original whole-number amounts and sources to support recalculation.

Product-specific revenue, undisclosed customer purchase amounts, complete supplier shares, real-time institutional holdings, and consistent market-wide shares not provided in the materials have not been estimated. The report explains the business and disclosed financial results without investment ratings, price targets, or the author's forecasts of performance.

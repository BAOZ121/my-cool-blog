<p>Public information through 19 October 2026. Global scope, with China,
the United States and Europe examined in depth. Cargo aircraft, delivery
operations, landing and charging facilities, communications, navigation,
surveillance and airspace coordination form the core. Passenger eVTOL,
military aircraft and consumer photography are adjacent context.
“Low-altitude logistics” is an analytical scope here, not a uniform
international legal altitude category.</p>

<p>The industry's commercial test is whether aircraft, ground
facilities, permissions and demand can work together repeatedly at an
acceptable cost. This report separates completed deliveries from
flights, operating sites from planned sites, and service contribution
from company profit. Disclosed company activity is presented with its
original period and geography. It does not establish a global revenue
total or a supplier-share ranking.</p>

## Part 1: Industry Story {#part-1-industry-story}

### 1. A blood order explains the value of a network {#a-blood-order-explains-the-value-of-a-network}

<p>Rwanda began working with Zipline on blood deliveries in 2016. A
subsequent study examined 12,733 blood-product orders delivered between
March 2017 and December 2019 to 20 facilities; 43% were emergency
orders. Mean drone delivery time, including preparation and packaging,
was 49.6 minutes. The study associated the intervention with a 67%
reduction in blood-product expirations at 12 months. It was
observational: road travel times were estimated, and neither patient
outcomes nor cost effectiveness was established. <a href="#source-G15" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G15">[G15]</a></p>

<p>The useful change was broader than flying over a difficult road. A
central stock of short-lived products could respond to requests from
multiple hospitals. Hospitals could obtain particular blood components
when needed rather than hold every possible item locally. The commercial
lesson is an author inference: time savings can have value through
inventory, availability and emergency response, even where the flight
itself is expensive. That value depends on the commodity and the
alternative service.</p>

<p>Retail delivery tests another form of coordination. In September
2026, Meituan disclosed more than one million cumulative commercial
orders and the launch of a second systematic city network in Shanghai's
West Bund, following Shenzhen. Its announcement indicates a move from
individual routes toward connected ground facilities and dispatch. It
does not disclose an audited network profit. <a href="#source-C08" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C08">[C08]</a></p>

### 2. What makes these operations a network? {#what-makes-these-operations-a-network}

<p>For this report, a drone network has five connected layers: a
customer ordering system; physical collection and delivery points;
aircraft and charging or battery handling; flight planning and airspace
coordination; and people and software responsible for exceptions. This
is an analytical framework, not a claim that every operator has the same
architecture.</p>

<p>A successful flight is one event within that system. A parcel may
still wait for picking, loading, an available aircraft, clearance,
suitable weather or a recipient. The service succeeds when the right
goods reach the right destination reliably and the operator can account
for the full cost. This distinction links the industry's history to its
present business models.</p>

## Part 2: Industry History {#part-2-industry-history}

### 1. From a controlled task to a continuing service {#from-a-controlled-task-to-a-continuing-service}

<p>The 2016 Rwanda deployment showed the importance of an institutional
customer, defined delivery points and a central fulfilment system. It
was a particular medical supply chain, not proof that every household
delivery would be economical. The later independent study provided
evidence for delivery time and wastage within that setting. <a href="#source-G15" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G15">[G15]</a></p>

<p>In the United States, Part 107 created a framework for small
unmanned-aircraft operations, while package delivery developed through
additional permissions and air-carrier certification. The FAA records
Wing's Part 135 certification in April 2019 and UPS Flight Forward's in
September 2019. Aircraft certification, operating authority and
permission for a specific operation remain distinct steps. <a href="#source-G01" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G01">[G01]</a></p>

### 2. Europe develops risk categories and shared-airspace services {#europe-develops-risk-categories-and-shared-airspace-services}

<p>Europe's framework separates open, specific and certified operations
by risk. Operations that cannot meet the open-category conditions
commonly require a specific-category authorisation or another applicable
route. “Specific” does not mean that every aircraft must follow the same
type-certification path. <a href="#source-G06" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G06">[G06]</a></p>

<p>The U-space regulatory package became applicable on 26 January 2023.
It establishes services and responsibilities within designated U-space
airspace, rather than making every European area available for routine
delivery. In May 2025, EASA announced its first U-space service-provider
certificate, for ANRA Technologies. A service-provider certificate
concerns the provider's functions; it is not a logistics operator's
blanket flight approval. <a href="#source-G04" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G04">[G04]</a><a href="#source-G05" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G05">[G05]</a></p>

### 3. China puts operations within a national framework {#china-puts-operations-within-a-national-framework}

<p>China's Interim Regulations on the Flight Administration of Unmanned
Aircraft took effect on 1 January 2024. They define aircraft categories
and responsibilities for registration, operators, controlled airspace
and flight activities. CCAR-92 is the associated civil operating-safety
framework. The practical route depends on aircraft class, activity and
operating conditions; the phrase “low-altitude economy” does not itself
confer a right to fly. <a href="#source-C02" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C02">[C02]</a><a href="#source-C04" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C04">[C04]</a></p>

<p>The revised Civil Aviation Law took effect on 1 July 2026. It embeds
unmanned-aircraft airworthiness requirements and unique product
identification in the law while retaining prescribed exemptions and the
relationship with special drone rules. It should not be read as
requiring the same type certificate for every small aircraft. China also
published an October 2026 data-interface standard for the national
management platform and service-provider systems; the publication is a
standardisation milestone, not evidence that all providers have
completed integration. <a href="#source-C03" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C03">[C03]</a><a href="#source-C06" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C06">[C06]</a></p>

<p>Physical networks have developed alongside the rules. Meituan's 2026
M-Port 3 announcement describes a compact ground facility and a
service-provider programme. Fengyi's disclosed Greater Bay Area network
illustrates parcel consolidation across repeated routes. These are
different operating designs, rather than successive stages that every
company must follow. <a href="#source-C07" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C07">[C07]</a><a href="#source-C09" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C09">[C09]</a></p>

### 4. The next constraint is coordination at scale {#the-next-constraint-is-coordination-at-scale}

<p>NASA's December 2024 account of Dallas operations described multiple
operators sharing approved airspace through service-provider
coordination. This was a bounded implementation, not a universal
traffic-management service. Its importance is that independent networks
must exchange enough intent information to avoid incompatible plans. <a href="#source-T01" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T01">[T01]</a></p>

<p>The FAA proposed a broader beyond-visual-line-of-sight framework in
August 2025. A September 2026 TSA notice still described agencies as
finalising the work. This report treats the examined Part 108 material
as a proposal, not an effective final rule. Meanwhile, operations
continue under their applicable approvals and conditions. <a href="#source-G02" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G02">[G02]</a><a href="#source-G03" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G03">[G03]</a></p>

<p>This history explains the industry's mixed maturity. A company may
have an aircraft design, another may have commercial demand, and a third
may provide airspace services. Regular delivery requires these
capabilities to coincide at the same location. Progress in one layer
does not automatically remove constraints in the others.</p>

## Part 3: Market Landscape {#part-3-market-landscape}

### 1. Define the product before measuring the market {#define-the-product-before-measuring-the-market}

<p>At least four markets sit within the broad label. Retail and food
delivery transport small, urgent baskets to a pickup point or customer.
Medical logistics moves blood products, medicines or specimens within a
controlled chain of custody. Parcel and industrial cargo operations
consolidate goods between facilities or cross difficult terrain.
Infrastructure and software suppliers sell aircraft, ground equipment,
integration or coordination services to the operators.</p>

<p>These markets have different customers and denominators. Aircraft
sales measure equipment demand. Delivery fees measure transport service.
A government infrastructure contract can include construction and
software. Adding these revenues can count the same final demand more
than once. A consumer basket's value is not the operator's logistics
revenue. This report therefore uses disclosed operating evidence and
defined business segments rather than a blended global market-size
estimate.</p>

<p>China's 2025 aviation statistics give useful surrounding context:
3.287 million registered drones and 45.3029 million flight hours across
civil unmanned-aircraft uses. They include activities beyond logistics.
They cannot be used as a delivery-market denominator, or compared
directly with American operator registrations or European
authorisations. <a href="#source-C01" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C01">[C01]</a></p>

<p>Shenzhen provides a narrower local observation. Its 2026 government
work report states that cargo-drone flights exceeded one million in the
2025 review and that more than 1,200 low-altitude landing and takeoff
facilities had been built and put into use. The facilities cover uses
beyond logistics; flight counts are neither parcel counts nor revenue.
The report does not supply a national logistics total. <a href="#source-C12" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C12">[C12]</a></p>

### 2. The value chain and the money flowing through it {#the-value-chain-and-the-money-flowing-through-it}

<p>Upstream suppliers provide aircraft structures, propulsion and energy
systems, flight control, navigation, communications and sensing. A
product's payload and endurance must be read under the same test
configuration. DJI's FlyCart 30 and FlyCart 100 illustrate
cargo-aircraft offerings; their manufacturer specifications do not
establish achieved service range on a particular route. <a href="#source-T03" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T03">[T03]</a><a href="#source-T04" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T04">[T04]</a></p>

<p>Larger middle-mile aircraft address a different task. Dronamics
reported the Black Swan's first full-scale flight in Bulgaria in May
2023 and advertised a 350 kg payload and range of up to 2,500 km. These
are a development milestone and manufacturer specifications, not
verified route economics. Larger regional cargo systems may also operate
beyond low-altitude airspace. Their airfield and consolidated-load model
should be evaluated separately from urban last-mile delivery. <a href="#source-R01" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source R01">[R01]</a></p>

<p>Midstream businesses integrate aircraft, ground stations, software
and operational procedures into a deliverable system. Matternet supplies
a medical-logistics platform and also describes delivery-as-a-service.
Meituan's M-Port 3 represents the ground-interface layer. U-space and
UTM providers support defined coordination functions. These businesses
can sell equipment, installations, subscriptions, support or a
contracted operating service. <a href="#source-G13" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G13">[G13]</a><a href="#source-C07" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C07">[C07]</a><a href="#source-G05" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G05">[G05]</a><a href="#source-T01" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T01">[T01]</a></p>

<p>Downstream operators connect those capabilities to customers. Zipline
serves medical and retail uses; Wing works with retail partners; Meituan
integrates drone services with local commerce; Fengyi sits within the
parcel-delivery chain. A company can span several stages. The map groups
roles and examples; its links do not imply that one named company
supplies another. <a href="#source-G09" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G09">[G09]</a><a href="#source-G07" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G07">[G07]</a><a href="#source-C08" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C08">[C08]</a><a href="#source-C09" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C09">[C09]</a></p>

<p>Money need not follow the same path in every model. A hospital or

### 3. What the disclosed activity shows {#what-the-disclosed-activity-shows}

<p>The comparison graphic preserves the original metrics. Zipline
reported more than two million cumulative commercial deliveries in
January 2026. Wing reported well over one million in June. Meituan
reported more than one million cumulative commercial orders in
September. Their starting dates, geographies and delivery definitions
differ. These observations show sustained activity, but they cannot be
added together to produce a whole-market total. <a href="#source-G09" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G09">[G09]</a><a href="#source-G08" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G08">[G08]</a><a href="#source-C08" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C08">[C08]</a></p>

<p>Fengyi's Greater Bay Area disclosure reports more than 5.15 million
items and more than 600,000 flights cumulatively through 31 December
2024. An item is not a flight: one movement can carry multiple parcels,
and a flight can serve positioning or return needs. Matternet's May 2026
disclosure refers to more than 60,000 flights, including commercial,
humanitarian and test flights. These figures are useful within their own
networks, not as substitutes for each other's order counts. <a href="#source-C09" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C09">[C09]</a><a href="#source-G13" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G13">[G13]</a></p>

<p>JD Logistics' 2026 interim report adds a rural-network example. It
reported more than 100 domestic drone routes in operation and a June
launch in Zizhong, Sichuan covering 78 administrative villages. Its
fastest-delivery claim is not an average, and its consolidated logistics
revenue is not drone revenue. This is evidence of network deployment
rather than a comparable delivery-volume measure. <a href="#source-C14" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C14">[C14]</a></p>

### 4. China, the United States and Europe follow different operating paths {#china-the-united-states-and-europe-follow-different-operating-paths}

<p>China combines a national flight-administration framework with civil
operating rules and location-specific implementation. Platforms and
parcel networks can bring recurring demand and ground resources, but
route availability still depends on operating permissions and local
conditions. Meituan's city-network approach and Fengyi's parcel
connections illustrate two customer bases. Neither establishes uniform
access across China. <a href="#source-C02" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C02">[C02]</a><a href="#source-C04" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C04">[C04]</a><a href="#source-C08" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C08">[C08]</a><a href="#source-C09" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C09">[C09]</a></p>

<p>The United States combines federal aviation approvals with an
expanding retail-site network. Part 135 certification supports relevant
commercial delivery operations; aircraft and BVLOS permissions must
still match the actual service. Retail partnerships can reduce the
difficulty of finding dispatch locations and goods to carry. They do not
ensure that nearby demand fills the available flying hours. <a href="#source-G01" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G01">[G01]</a><a href="#source-G07" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G07">[G07]</a></p>

<p>Europe's risk-based framework and U-space services offer common
regulatory building blocks, but authorisations, geography and commercial
deployment remain uneven. Ireland-based Manna's July 2026 announcement
reported almost 380,000 cumulative deliveries globally. It is one
company's experience, not an estimate for Ireland or Europe. UK
requirements are a separate national regime and are not treated here as
EU permissions. <a href="#source-G06" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G06">[G06]</a><a href="#source-G04" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G04">[G04]</a><a href="#source-G10" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G10">[G10]</a></p>

### 5. How a route becomes an economic service {#how-a-route-becomes-an-economic-service}

<p>The appropriate denominator is a successfully completed delivery
under the promised service level. The following is an author framework,
not a reported industry-average cost model:</p>

<p>Cost per completed delivery = (allocated fixed network cost + flight
and maintenance cost + ground handling + expected exception and fallback
cost) / completed deliveries.</p>

<p>Fixed cost includes aircraft and ground-facility capital recovery,
software, insurance, compliance and the staff needed to keep a site
available. Variable cost includes energy, component and battery wear,
loading, delivery handling and incremental support. Contract design
determines which party bears each cost. Allocating them to completed
deliveries also exposes the burden of cancellations, empty repositioning
and standby capacity.</p>

<p>Network density helps when repeated demand uses the same
infrastructure. It can also create queues at loading points, battery
stations or delivery areas. More aircraft improve throughput only until
another resource becomes limiting. Similarly, a larger service radius
can bring more demand while increasing cycle time and reducing the
number of journeys each aircraft can perform. These are analytical
tradeoffs; the report assigns no universal optimum radius or fleet
size.</p>

<p>An operator should examine the complete cycle: order release,
picking, loading, launch, outbound flight, handover, return or
repositioning, inspection and recharge. Straight-line flight speed
measures only one part. Weather availability and the frequency of
difficult handovers affect how much nominal capacity is actually
usable.</p>

### 6. Contribution, cash flow and company profit {#contribution-cash-flow-and-company-profit}

<p>A route can cover incremental flight cost while failing to recover
the network's fixed cost. A mature site can be profitable while a
company spends heavily opening new sites. Positive unit economics,
operating profit, net profit and positive cash flow therefore require
separate evidence.</p>

<p>Manna's April 2026 announcement claimed positive unit economics for
residential last-mile delivery. That is a company claim and does not
demonstrate audited group profitability. Zipline's January announcement
disclosed more than $600 million of funding at a $7.6 billion valuation.
Financing and valuation are not customer revenue. <a href="#source-G11" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G11">[G11]</a><a href="#source-G09" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G09">[G09]</a></p>

<p>Matternet offers a more explicit financial boundary. Its Form 10-Q
for the nine months ended 30 June 2026 reported revenue of $255,564 and
an operating loss of $10,642,176. These are one reporting entity's
results for that nine-month period, not a global drone-logistics margin.
Its enterprise platform-access and delivery-service model also differs
from a retail operator's per-order model. <a href="#source-G12" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G12">[G12]</a></p>

<p>For a simplified service with fixed cost F, realised revenue per
delivery p and incremental cost v, break-even completed volume would be
F / (p − v), provided p exceeds v. This is an author equation, not a
forecast. It assumes a stable mix and sufficient physical capacity. A
route with a positive contribution margin can still be unable to achieve
the required volume within weather, demand and operating limits.</p>

<p>Single-route savings need a similarly explicit boundary. In its Wuxi
case, SF described a 1.87 km connection taking about 2.5 minutes and
claimed 30% lower transport cost than the previous arrangement. The
service retained ground collection and doorstep delivery. Without a
complete baseline for capital, utilisation, handling and support, that
claim cannot establish an industry-average cost saving. <a href="#source-C10" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C10">[C10]</a></p>

<p>Public support can change the calculation. Shenzhen's applicable
rules offer conditional route and flight-volume awards, with an annual
logistics-award cap of RMB20 million per operator. That ceiling is
neither cash received nor customer revenue. A business case should show
commercial contribution separately from qualifying awards and assess
what happens when those awards expire. <a href="#source-C13" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C13">[C13]</a></p>

### 7. Where durable competitive strength may emerge {#where-durable-competitive-strength-may-emerge}

<p>The strongest position may combine permitted operations, dependable
equipment, accessible ground sites and repeat demand. A manufacturer's
certification knowledge can reduce customers' deployment friction. A
retailer's order flow can improve utilisation. An operator's maintenance
and exception records can improve planning. Interoperable coordination
can make shared airspace easier to use. Each advantage still needs a
commercial mechanism that allows its owner to capture value.</p>

<p>The report's assessment is that recurring medical routes, difficult
terrain and carefully selected retail catchments deserve separate
evaluation. Medical value can include avoided inventory losses; an
industrial customer may pay for an otherwise slow crossing; retail
demand may support frequent operations near a dispatch site. None is
automatically the largest or most profitable segment. Procurement
prices, alternatives and actual utilisation decide the outcome.</p>

## Part 4: Industry Challenges {#part-4-industry-challenges}

### 1. Safety must survive abnormal operation {#safety-must-survive-abnormal-operation}

<p>Safe routine flight is only the starting point. Operators must plan
for lost communication, degraded navigation, energy shortfalls,
unexpected aircraft, people entering a delivery area and failed landing
or release mechanisms. The FAA/NASA conflict-management model separates
strategic coordination from tactical responses. A coordinated plan
cannot replace the aircraft's ability to handle an immediate hazard. <a href="#source-T02" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T02">[T02]</a></p>

<p>The NTSB's final WPR25LA103 report records a February 2025 Amazon
MK30 accident during a Part 91 flight test in Pendleton, Oregon. The
controlled scenario included an intentionally placed obstacle and
another drone. The NTSB found failure to maintain obstacle clearance.
This is not evidence of a routine customer-delivery failure rate;
comparable safety rates require flight-exposure data and consistent
event definitions. <a href="#source-G14" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G14">[G14]</a></p>

<p>The commercial response is to link technical safeguards to a defined
operating envelope and a workable recovery procedure. Recovery staff,
reserve aircraft and fallback transport add cost, but omitting them can
make an apparently cheap service unreliable. This is an operational
inference, not an allegation about an individual provider.</p>

### 2. Airspace coordination is a system dependency {#airspace-coordination-is-a-system-dependency}

<p>UTM and U-space services can exchange plans, provide information and
support authorisation or coordination. Their exact responsibilities
depend on the regulatory and operational design. A provider can only
coordinate from the information and connectivity available to it.
Congestion, stale data, uncooperative aircraft and service outages still
require explicit procedures. <a href="#source-T01" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T01">[T01]</a><a href="#source-T02" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T02">[T02]</a><a href="#source-G04" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source G04">[G04]</a></p>

<p>Communications, navigation and surveillance are complementary
functions. A cellular connection does not by itself establish a safe
command link throughout a route, and a navigation fix does not establish
that nearby traffic has been detected. The analytical implication is
that infrastructure procurement should specify performance, outage
behaviour and accountability, rather than assume that installing a
network technology guarantees flight capacity.</p>

### 3. Payload, weather and ground work constrain productivity {#payload-weather-and-ground-work-constrain-productivity}

<p>Manufacturer maximum-payload, maximum-range and
environmental-resistance figures refer to defined configurations and
test conditions. They cannot be combined freely. A single-battery
emergency figure, for example, is not the same configuration as normal
dual-battery operation. Route planning must retain reserve energy and
account for wind, elevation and handling. <a href="#source-T03" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T03">[T03]</a><a href="#source-T04" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T04">[T04]</a></p>

<p>Ground automation can remove repetitive work, but it also introduces
machinery, maintenance and interfaces between retailers, operators and
recipients. Meituan's M-Port 3 announcement is evidence of investment in
that layer; its performance and cost claims remain company disclosures.
A compact footprint alone does not establish total installation cost or
throughput at every site. <a href="#source-C07" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source C07">[C07]</a></p>

<p>The operating question is how much labour remains per completed
delivery across the whole chain. Remote supervision ratios, loading
productivity and exception frequency matter as much as flight autonomy.
Customers need service continuity during adverse weather, whether that
means road fallback, a revised promise or an explicit suspension
policy.</p>

### 4. Community acceptance affects usable capacity {#community-acceptance-affects-usable-capacity}

<p>Aircraft can be technically capable of more flights than a community
finds acceptable. NASA's laboratory noise research examined human
responses to drone and road-vehicle sounds. Laboratory annoyance
findings do not establish a universal urban exposure limit. They do
support treating the character of sound, repetition and operating
context as issues beyond a single headline decibel value. <a href="#source-T05" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T05">[T05]</a></p>

<p>Delivery-area safety, cameras, personal information and complaints
also require accountable processes. For the operator, a useful response
is to provide clear delivery instructions, incident reporting,
data-access controls and a contact channel. These are analytical
recommendations. The report does not assume that one region's aviation
approval resolves local land-use, privacy or public-acceptance
questions.</p>

### 5. Environmental benefit depends on the whole service {#environmental-benefit-depends-on-the-whole-service}

<p>An electric flight is not a complete environmental assessment.
Payload, route length, the electricity mix, batteries, additional
facilities and the road service displaced can change the result. The
examined 2018 life-cycle study and its correction support this
conditional interpretation. The report does not extrapolate an
experimental small-drone saving to every cargo aircraft or city network.
<a href="#source-T07" class="evidence-cite" aria-label="Source T07">[T07]</a></p>

<p>A fair comparison should hold the delivery task constant. Replacing a
dedicated urgent road trip differs from removing one small parcel from
an already busy van. New fast-delivery demand can also add trips.
Environmental reporting should therefore state the baseline, the
allocation method and the network boundary alongside any claimed
reduction.</p>

### 6. Expansion needs evidence of repeatable service {#expansion-needs-evidence-of-repeatable-service}

<p>Funding, announced sites and technically possible range make
expansion possible. They do not establish repeatable returns. Public
buyers also need to distinguish payment for a demonstration, payment for
availability and payment for completed deliveries. A successful pilot
can answer a technical question without establishing the economics of
permanent deployment.</p>

<p>The following indicators provide a practical review framework:
completed paid deliveries per active site; repeat customer use; service
completion and cancellation rates; end-to-end delivery time; aircraft
and ground-facility utilisation; labour across the full delivery cycle;
maintenance and exception cost; safety events with exposure measures;
and contribution and cash requirements under an explicit accounting
boundary. These are proposed indicators, not claimed industry
disclosures.</p>

<p>As of the cutoff, the strongest evidence supports a collection of
real operating networks with different missions and commercial
structures. The next stage depends on whether they can reproduce
dependable service at new locations while keeping demand, permissions,
infrastructure and costs aligned. That is the test that connects a
successful aircraft to a sustainable logistics business.</p>

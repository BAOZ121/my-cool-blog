Therapeutic Peptides and the GLP-1 Value Chain

From ingredients to chronic care: where value moves after scale-up

Version 1.0 | Global scope with attention to the Chinese supply chain | Research cutoff: 21 October 2026. Annual, moving-annual-total and half-year observations are labelled separately. “Biological peptides” here means therapeutic peptides and their pharmaceutical supply chain, excluding food, cosmetic, collagen-hydrolysate and unapproved wellness-peptide markets. The GLP-1 scope includes single-receptor agonists and dual/multiple agonists with GLP-1 activity. Non-peptide GLP-1 medicines are discussed only as competitive substitutes. This is industry research, not medical, prescribing or investment advice.

P1 Industry Story

Three factories explain more than one injection

On 18 December 2024, Novo Nordisk completed the acquisition of three manufacturing sites from Novo Holdings. The parties matter: Novo Holdings acquired Catalent; Novo Nordisk acquired three of its fill-finish sites. In June 2026, Catalent was still correcting reports that incorrectly treated the Bloomington facility, now owned and operated by Novo Nordisk, as part of its own network. [E01][E02]
Why would a company with successful medicines compete for filling plants? An approved molecule must still become a formulated, aseptically filled, tested, released and distributed product. A slow expansion at any of these stages can prevent upstream synthesis capacity from becoming sales. The acquisition does not establish a site's GLP-1 output, but it demonstrates the strategic importance of controlling downstream manufacturing.
On 21 February 2025, the US FDA determined that the national semaglutide-injection shortage was resolved while acknowledging possible local interruptions. [E03] Aggregate supply and a patient's ability to obtain one presentation at one pharmacy are different problems. The commercial challenge extends from making a molecule to supporting continuous, compliant and affordable care.

Separate three markets before counting them

Therapeutic peptides are medicines with peptide active ingredients and the manufacturing services behind them; GLP-1 is an important subset. GLP-1-related medicines are a mechanism-based category that can include both peptides and non-peptide small molecules. Obesity medicines are an indication-based category. The same molecule can appear in different brands, formulations and indications. These categories overlap without being interchangeable.
The distinction has immediate commercial consequences in 2026. US approval of oral Wegovy demonstrates that oral delivery does not mean a non-peptide molecule. Foundayo (orforglipron), by contrast, is an approved non-peptide oral GLP-1 medicine: it may compete for patients without creating the same peptide-input demand. [M03][M04] Authorisations remain product-, indication- and jurisdiction-specific; clinical results are not global marketing permission.

Large clinical need is not a revenue forecast

WHO's October 2026 fact sheet estimates that 940 million adults lived with obesity in 2024, about 16% of adults worldwide. [E04] This establishes the scale of public-health need, not an immediately addressable pharmaceutical market. Diagnosis, authorised indications, contraindications, affordability, reimbursement, clinical capacity and persistence all affect actual treatment numbers.
The central argument of this report is that the industry is moving beyond increasing supply toward qualifying it, sustaining its use and securing payment. Peptide suppliers depend on product mix, ingredient requirements per treated patient, outsourcing decisions, prices and yields. Branded manufacturers also depend on clinical evidence and market access. Strong end-market demand and factory-level margin pressure can coexist.

P2 Industry Value Chain

Upstream: inputs and the evidence that travels with them

Chemical routes begin with protected amino acids, solid supports, coupling and deprotection reagents, solvents and related inputs. Suppliers provide batch consistency, impurity control, reliable deliveries and traceable documentation as well as materials. Changing an input can affect a commercial manufacturing process; a cheaper substitute is not necessarily interchangeable. Verified representative activities appear in the map and appendix. Category connections are not confirmed supply contracts. [C01][C02]
Equipment spans synthesis vessels, preparative chromatography, filtration, concentration, drying, analytical instruments and consumables. Equipment sales, recurring consumables and services have different cash-flow patterns. A plant expansion can produce a large equipment order, while ongoing operation drives consumable demand. Reactor volume, resin loading and nominal capacity cannot be converted directly into finished-drug output.
Biological routes add cell systems, media, fermentation and biological separation. EMA's Ozempic product information identifies recombinant production in Saccharomyces cerevisiae cells. [E06] Peptides therefore cannot be reduced to a single solid-phase-synthesis market. The molecule, intellectual property and manufacturing route determine which suppliers and assets matter.

Midstream: turning a sequence into releasable API

Solid-phase synthesis assembles amino-acid units on a support before downstream processing. Industrial production also requires modification, purification, impurity characterisation, validation and release. Incomplete reactions and side products influence the difficulty of purification. Commercial value lies in reproducibly delivering material to the same specification, not simply making a sequence once. Products may use solid-phase, liquid-phase, fragment-based, recombinant or hybrid routes rather than one universal GLP-1 process. [C03][E06]
Bachem, PolyPeptide and CordenPharma illustrate specialist peptide manufacturing; relevant Chinese capability includes WuXi AppTec's WuXi TIDES platform. Company and segment definitions matter: TIDES includes oligonucleotides as well as peptides, so its revenue is not GLP-1 revenue. Demonstrated process capability does not establish a supply relationship with a named marketed brand. [C04][C05][C06][C12]
CDMO revenue can come from development, clinical batches, transfer, commercial production and capacity arrangements. Numerous development projects need not mean substantial near-term tonnage; a large commercial product can create concentration risk. Supplier transfers require technical and quality work, supporting customer retention. Customer insourcing, project failures and changing forecasts can nevertheless reduce utilisation.
Chinese participation extends beyond one platform. Asymchem discloses peptide programmes at development, validation and commercial stages and fragment-based processes; Sinopep-Allsino offers peptide API, CMC and formulation services. Project counts, mixed-segment revenue and actual GLP-1 output are not interchangeable. CordenPharma’s European and US investment plans demonstrate international expansion, but planned 2028 commercial starts cannot be counted as operating 2026 supply. [C09][C11][C12]

The manufacturing interface: formulation, filling and devices

After API manufacture, injections require formulation stability, container closure, aseptic processing, filling inspection and device reliability. Pens, prefilled syringes, cartridges and vials differ in dose accuracy, user steps, materials, supplier qualification and production-line compatibility. A device supplier's platform establishes a capability, not a procurement relationship with a particular brand. [C07]
Oral peptides reduce dependence on injection devices while shifting challenges toward stability, absorption and administration conditions. The 2025 Wegovy tablet label specifies a 25 mg daily maintenance dose and fasting requirements; in 2026 the FDA approved a higher 7.2 mg weekly injection. [E07][E08] These figures illustrate manufacturing demand, not prescribing advice or a clinical ranking.
The supply-chain implication is that similar patient numbers can require very different quantities of API and packaging. Prescription growth cannot simply be applied to peptide tonnage. A credible demand model needs formulation mix, persistence, ramp-up yields, inventory and the customer's outsourcing share.

Downstream: who determines access

Branded manufacturers integrate clinical development, registration, intellectual property, production networks and commercial access. Clinicians determine diagnosis and prescribing; payers and intermediaries influence coverage and patient cost; wholesalers, pharmacies and authorised online services deliver products. Their relative importance differs across health systems.
A branded medicine's selling price is not the API supplier's available revenue pool. It also supports research, unsuccessful programmes, evidence generation, compliance, commercialisation and manufacturing coordination. List prices, cash prices and manufacturer net realisations must not be mixed. WHO's 2025 guidance highlighted cost, health-system readiness and equitable access among the reasons for conditional recommendations. [E05] Payment and care delivery constrain effective demand alongside chemistry.

P3 Market Landscape

Comparable shares first, financial scale second

Novo Nordisk's FY 2025 report cites IQVIA data showing a 45.8% global GLP-1 diabetes value share for the moving annual total ending November 2025, versus 55.1% a year earlier, a decline of 9.3 percentage points. [M01] The donut therefore contains only Novo Nordisk, 45.8%, and other manufacturers combined, 54.2%. The residual is calculated as 100% minus 45.8%; it must not be labelled entirely as Lilly.
The denominator is the source-defined GLP-1 diabetes value market. International tirzepatide is classified as diabetes even where it also has an obesity indication, and some regional coverage is incomplete. This is not the whole obesity-drug, peptide API or CDMO market, nor audited manufacturer net sales. The historical period remains explicit rather than being presented as a live October 2026 share. [M01]
Company revenue gives a separate scale indicator. Novo reported FY 2025 GLP-1 diabetes sales of DKK 152.202 billion and obesity-care sales of DKK 82.347 billion. Lilly reported worldwide Mounjaro and Zepbound revenue of US$22.965 billion and US$13.542 billion, respectively, totalling US$36.507 billion. [M01][M02] Currencies are not combined, and brand-indication relationships differ by country. These figures cannot establish whole-value-chain shares.

Reading 2026 growth without mixing accounting and demand

Lilly's first-half 2026 Mounjaro and Zepbound revenue was US$18.605 billion and US$9.088 billion, totalling US$27.693 billion. Foundayo generated US$98 million. [M06] Revenue demonstrates that commercialisation has begun; a small launch-period base does not justify extrapolating long-term share.
Novo's first-half 2026 statements include a DKK 26.760 billion reversal of a US 340B provision. Total reported sales were DKK 175.311 billion, versus adjusted sales of DKK 148.551 billion. [M05] Reported revenue changes cannot all be interpreted as additional drug volumes, much less API demand. Accounting adjustments, currencies, net prices, mix and periods must be reconciled first.
For suppliers, the useful questions are how price and volume separate, how much manufacturing is captive, which molecular formats create demand, and whether channel growth reflects new patients, switching or inventory. These variables connect medicine sales to actual manufacturing orders.

Three competitive layers, not one company ranking

The first layer comprises originators with clinical evidence, authorisations, brands and access. Novo and Lilly also manufacture internally. The opportunity for outside suppliers is the demand available after captive production and other constraints, not all downstream medicine revenue.
The second layer comprises specialist manufacturing and process platforms. Quality systems, scale-up, delivery records and transfer capability win projects. Bachem's commercial GMP batches from a new building and PolyPeptide's utilisation ramp illustrate a shift from announcing assets to operating them. Manufacturer capacity statements are not independently measured industry shares. [C04][C05]
The third layer comprises new molecules, formulations, generics and regional competitors. The FDA's 2024 approval of the first generic referencing Victoza demonstrates an established-product pathway, not identical development difficulty or market rights across all peptides. [E09] Novo still described CagriSema as investigational on 20 October 2026; retatrutide also cannot be assigned an approved-product sales share. [M07][E10]
Chinese process-development and manufacturing capability is distinct from competition in China's finished-drug market. Growth at WuXi TIDES supports the former, not automatic leadership in the latter. [C06] Nor does expiry of a compound patent eliminate all process, formulation, use or regulatory barriers. [C08]

Geography changes the business model

The United States illustrates interactions among commercial scale, net prices, insurance and direct-to-patient channels, but its shortage and compounding rules are not global rules. In Europe, EU authorisation must be separated from country-level access, reimbursement and launch. China combines an innovative-drug market, manufacturing services and domestic competition.
A global forecast therefore needs a region-by-product matrix. Lower prices can expand coverage while reducing net revenue per unit; an authorisation elsewhere may precede meaningful sales. For Chinese suppliers, international capability requires verifiable quality, regulatory support, customer relationships and local compliance, not simply export orders. No single price, patent date or regional prescription trend represents the world.

P4 Industry Problems

After expansion, utilisation and unit cost matter

Bachem reported commercial GMP batches from Building K in the first half of 2026, while ramp-up costs weighed on profitability: its EBITDA margin fell to 25.4% from 29.1%. PolyPeptide’s half-year 2026 report says its Belgian large-scale solid-phase-synthesis plant operated at target utilisation, while other sites still had assets in commissioning or ramp-up. [C04][C05] These are company examples, not industry averages, but they contradict the assumption that completed capacity immediately releases profit.
Training, validation, commissioning and fixed costs precede the gradual increase in saleable batches. Synthesis, purification, analysis and release can become mismatched. Staged expansion, process improvement, appropriate long-term contracts and customer diversification can help; commitments can also lock in unattractive prices or the wrong mix.
Diligence should examine released batches, qualified output, utilisation, failures and reprocessing, delivery and cash collection rather than vessel litres, floor space or announced spending alone. Where comparable data are absent, a blank is more informative than a manufactured global share.

Quality, environmental burden and resilience remain responsibilities

Scale-up means more than enlarging a vessel. Impurities can resemble the target molecule, while purification consumes time, solvents and equipment capacity. Larger batches increase the consequences of a deviation. Lower-solvent and improved routes can benefit both cost and environmental performance, provided quality remains demonstrated. [C03]
Compliance and process capability create opportunity and risk for Chinese and other international suppliers. FDA's GLP-1 API import alert focuses on quality and manufacturing compliance rather than prohibiting all foreign API. [E10] Assessment should be site-, product- and status-specific, not based on nationality alone.
Second sources, critical-input inventories and alternative routes can improve resilience but require qualification and cost. Resilience does not mean duplicating every asset. Scarce materials, specialist equipment, concentrated customers and single-site dependence need different buffers.
Hybio describes work at its Wuhan site to segregate and recover waste from synthesis, cleavage and purification. This provides a concrete example of disposal infrastructure becoming a condition for expanding crude-peptide output. The announcement is a project account, not a quantified realised saving. [C10]

Access and persistence constrain the demand model

The end market is not simply the population with obesity multiplied by one year's list price. Clinical need does not ensure diagnosis, reimbursement or continuous prescriptions, and treatment initiation does not ensure full-year persistence. WHO included affordability, health-system capacity and long-term evidence among its considerations. [E05] Access is part of demand conversion, not a footnote added after a sales forecast.
Clinical competition also cannot be ranked by weight-loss percentages from unrelated trials. Populations, follow-up, diabetes status, estimands and treatment-discontinuation handling differ. Industrial analysis should consider tolerability, follow-up, persistence and disease outcomes rather than treating promotional summaries or simple cross-trial comparisons as head-to-head evidence.
For suppliers this creates ordering variability: starts generate initial demand, maintenance drives continued consumption, and payment and formulation changes alter mix. Scenarios should vary these drivers separately rather than apply one growth rate to the entire industry.

Generics, compounding and research products are distinct

Approved generics follow an applicable approval pathway; compounded medicines do not undergo FDA premarket review for safety, effectiveness and quality. FDA's public warnings address dosing errors, shipping conditions and misrepresented sources, and identify retatrutide and cagrilintide as not being components of FDA-approved medicines. [E09][E10] Online “research-use” sales should not be counted as ordinary compliant finished-drug demand or treated as equivalent approved alternatives.
Changes following shortage resolution also distinguish demand based on temporary regulatory conditions from demand supported by enduring registrations and clinical evidence. [E03] This does not mean that every compounded preparation is unlawful: legality depends on specific conditions, actors and products.
Intellectual property is similarly layered. Novo's disclosed compound-patent dates do not extinguish every other right, and Chinese litigation involves disputed extended exclusivity; the disclosed allegations are party claims, not final judicial findings. [C08] Expiry is not a guarantee that any competitor can immediately launch without country-, patent- and regulatory-level checks.

Conclusion: prove the value of capacity

Long-term opportunity comes from large chronic-disease needs and continued innovation in drug formats, but value will not accrue equally to every capacity expansion. Originators compete on evidence and access; process platforms on quality and delivery; input and equipment suppliers on dependable standardised supply; channels on continuous, compliant service.
Five signals deserve attention: qualified output and utilisation at new plants; the actual mix of injectable peptides, oral peptides and non-peptide medicines; net prices and coverage; jurisdiction-specific registrations and rights; and suppliers' ability to build durable customer portfolios. These signals must be connected before treating GLP-1 demand as a guarantee of supplier profit.
